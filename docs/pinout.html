The board as it really sits — both GPIO headers in physical pin order. The joystick and buttons wire to the header pins; the display plugs into the UEXT connector beside it. One wiring runs on both modules, so the two recall buttons share GPIO34 as a single analog input (one resistor) and GPIO16 is left free (the WROVER's PSRAM owns it). Colours mark what each pin does. Wiring

The board & the UEXT connector

Room to expand? Not on the dual-module wiring.

The two pins that look spare are each taken by the WROVER — so for one wiring that runs on both modules, leave both empty:

  • GPIO0 — free on a WROOM, but on a WROVER it carries the Ethernet 50 MHz clock (the WROVER clocks ETH out on GPIO0 because PSRAM took GPIO16/17). A button there would break Ethernet. Even on a WROOM it's a boot-strap pin — pressed during power-up/reset it drops the chip into firmware-download mode — so it's a poor home for a frequently-used control.
  • GPIO16 — also free on a WROOM only. On a WROVER the module's PSRAM owns GPIO16/17, so a button there breaks the module.
  • GPIO1 and GPIO3 are the USB serial console (UART0 TX/RX, through the CH340). Free only if you give up serial logging and USB flashing — you'd rely on OTA updates instead.
  • GPIO2 is the display backlight (via UEXT) and itself a strapping pin — not available.
  • Everything else is taken (joystick, buttons, display) or committed: GPIO34/35/36/39 are input-only (joystick axes + the recall buttons), GPIO39 also reads the supply voltage, and the rest of the chip is the Ethernet PHY. To add controls you'd combine more buttons onto the existing ladder, add an I²C/ADC expander, or free up the serial pins. (On a WROOM-only build, GPIO0 and GPIO16 open back up.)

Joystick detail

Three identical, unlabeled pots and a stick that mounts either way — so wire by colour and motion, never by which pot "looks like" pan, then prove it on the display radar before install.

Green = Pan · GPIO33 Yellow = Tilt · GPIO35 Blue = Zoom · GPIO36 Red = VCC 3V3 White = GND

3Wire it — one power rail, three wipers

The one thing that silently bites you: keep every pot's VCC on the left / GND on the right (the polarity card above) as you chain them. It's easy to flip a pot end-for-end while soldering the daisy-chain — and a flipped pot inverts that axis. If two axes come out swapped, you crossed the green/yellow wipers; if one axis is reversed, you flipped that pot's outer legs.

Testing

Prove the board & buttons before you install

Orientation (best-guess reference — the radar is the real judge): mount the controller so the operator's “up” on the stick is physically up and the wire lead exits toward the operator. Because Pan/Tilt were identified by motion (card 1), the exact mount doesn't change the wiring — but it must match what the operator expects, and the test below confirms it.
  1. Power the controller and look at the radar on its display.
  2. Push the stick UP → the dot must go UP. Push RIGHT → dot RIGHT. Push LEFT/DOWN → matching.
  3. Twist zoom in → the zoom bar fills up; twist out → it empties.
  4. Buttons: press Recall 1, Recall 2 and Override in turn. Recall 1 & 2 share GPIO34 — watch the serial log line recall ladder ADC=…: rest ≈ 4095, Recall 2 ≈ 2048, Recall 1 ≈ 0 (onboard BUT1 ≈ 88 also reads as Recall 1). Both at once → ≈ 0 = Recall 1 (its hard short wins; no both-press dead zone). Each button must land in its own band, and Override (its own pin, GPIO32) must read separately.
  5. All four directions, zoom, and every button correct → ship it. Anything wrong → the table:
